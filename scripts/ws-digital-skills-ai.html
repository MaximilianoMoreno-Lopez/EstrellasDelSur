Workshop 2: Digital Skills & Artificial Intelligence

Two back-to-back workshops: getting comfortable with everyday digital tools, and using AI to make sense of official paperwork.

8 participants Young men, 18+ Morocco & Senegal 2 facilitators

This session put two workshops side by side: one on digital skills and one on artificial intelligence. This time the group was eight young men from Morocco and Senegal, from the same group as the previous sessions. As we had already seen, most were confident on phones and social media but had little experience with computers, email, cloud storage and the kind of digital tools that come up when you are looking for work or dealing with paperwork.

Digital Skills workshop

We started with an energizer called "Molecules" to get people moving and chatting about the digital tools they use day to day. It set a good mood and got the group ready to work together.

The main activity was an introduction to Google Drive and Google Docs. We did not have a projector, so we ended up demonstrating directly on people's own devices, which made the explanations slower and trickier to follow. On top of that, the group's starting level meant a lot of one-to-one help, and with only two of us for eight participants it was not always easy to keep everyone moving at the same pace.

Working in pairs, participants put together a simple document about a sports event. Along the way they learned how to create and name a document, use basic formatting, work inside a shared folder, download a file as a PDF and send it by email. We had to trim the activity down because of time and the amount of support needed, but everyone got through the core tasks and came out more confident with tools that had been completely new to many of them.

Artificial Intelligence workshop

The second half looked at how AI can help you understand official documents and get through administrative procedures, something that affects this group directly.

First, people read a sample document with no tools at all, picking out the key bits: deadlines, what you are being asked to do, the details that matter. That alone showed just how hard official, bureaucratic language can be.

Then we brought in AI tools like ChatGPT and NotebookLM to summarise the document, explain the complicated words, pull out the important information and translate it into languages people were more comfortable with. They also played with different prompts and saw for themselves that clearer instructions get you clearer, more useful answers.

The part we cared about most was thinking critically. Participants compared what the AI said with the original document and we talked about why you always check before you trust it. The message we wanted to land was simple: AI is there to help you decide, not to decide for you.

What we took away

The session showed both what the group needs and what it is capable of. We did not tick off every objective of the digital skills workshop, mostly because of the limited prior experience and the missing projector, but everyone completed the essential tasks and clearly grew more confident with the tools.

The AI workshop went down especially well. People quickly saw how useful it could be for understanding official documents and handling the everyday admin that life throws at them. Taken together, the session moved the needle on digital confidence, independence and awareness of tools that can genuinely help with integration, employability and daily life.
